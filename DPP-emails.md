## EMAIL 2 — Approaching a potential DPP ⭐ SEND THIS ONE

~150 words. Everything a prescriber needs to decide whether to keep reading. The
University's full template is below as Email 3 — send that only if they ask for
detail, or attach the DPP Handbook instead.

**Subject:** Pharmacist prescribing student seeking a supervisor — 30 hrs, flexible, $200 honorarium

Dear Dr [Surname],

I'm a pharmacist here in Dubbo, studying the Graduate Certificate in Pharmacist
Prescribing at the University of Newcastle. I need a Designated Prescribing
Practitioner to supervise 30 hours of placement, and I'm hoping you might consider it.

In practice it means observing and learning alongside you, at times that suit your
schedule, any time between now and 13 November. You'd give feedback as we go and
sign off my hours at the end.

The University provides a handbook, approves the arrangement, and pays a $200
honorarium. I do all the coordinating and documentation — it's designed to cost you
as little time as possible.

Could I send you the outline, or call for five minutes to explain?

Kind regards,
[YOUR FULL NAME]
[YOUR PHONE]

**Why this works better than the template:** the ask is in the first paragraph, the
time commitment and deadline are concrete, and the last line is a five-minute
request rather than a decision. Nobody has to commit to anything to reply.

**If you'd rather not lead with money**, change the subject to *"Request to
supervise a pharmacist prescribing student — 30 hours, flexible"* and leave the
honorarium in the body. Keep it in either way — it signals this is a funded
University program, not a personal favour.

## Who actually qualifies (Handbook 1.2.1)

- Independent authorised prescriber: **GP, specialist, rural generalist, nurse
  practitioner, dental prescriber**, or pharmacist prescriber where recognised in
  the jurisdiction
- Current **unconditional AHPRA registration** — no conditions or undertakings on
  prescribing or supervision (the University verifies this)
- Relevant, current prescribing experience; confident supervising prescribing
- Their scope must align with what you'll be doing
- **No conflict of interest** — cannot be a relative, family member, close friend,
  or someone you're in a relationship with

Worth knowing: **there is no cap on how many students one DPP can supervise.** If
you find a willing GP, they can potentially take you and a classmate. That makes
the ask easier, not harder.

## Order of approach

1. **Placement team** — today. Email 1 above.
2. **Prescribers you already know professionally.** This is exactly what the
   Checklist asks for: *"Approach an authorised prescriber you already know."*
   GPs and NPs who call your pharmacy, whose scripts you query, who you have a
   working relationship with. Warm approaches convert; cold ones don't.
3. **Targeted cold approach** — a short list of Dubbo practices, phoned first,
   then Email 2 to a named person. Ask reception for the practice manager and
   whether any GP takes students. Ten good approaches beat a hundred blind ones,
   and Dubbo is small enough that how you approach people will be remembered.
