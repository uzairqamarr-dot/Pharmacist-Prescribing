## Why it's written this way

- **The table is the point.** She asked for a list so the team can see the effort is
  real and avoid duplicating it. Dates and outcomes make it verifiable; a paragraph
  saying "I've tried lots of places" does not.
- **The travel-and-hours line pre-empts her next question.** If she's matching you to
  a supervisor, the first thing she needs to know is how far your flexibility extends.
  Saying it unprompted removes a round trip.
- **Naming the tutorial strategies shows you acted on her advice.** She suggested
  watching that section; demonstrating you did, and what you took from it, is the
  difference between a student who needs managing and one worth spending effort on.
- **The AMS ask is the one concrete thing she can do today.** Requests get actioned
  when they're specific and small. "Please help me find a DPP" is neither.
- **Nothing is overstated.** If you haven't sent the DMAHG or Dubbo Health Hub emails
  yet, delete those rows rather than dating them — she may well contact the same
  practices, and a claim that doesn't hold up costs more than a short list.
