Lifestyle Recommendations for Hypertension

Record your notes on the top 5 recommendations for lifest\...

**\
Physical Activity**\
if 18-64:\
2.5 to 5 hours of moderate intensity exercise or 1.25 to 2.5 hours of
vigorous exercise per week\
Muscle strengthening activities on 2 days per week\
\
if \>65 years old:\
Some form of physical activity no matter what the age.\
30 minutes of moderate intensity exercise on preferably all days

**\
Weight Control**\
Set achievable intermediate goals in consultation with patients and
assess progress regularly.\
\
Convey the message that a small amount of weight loss can improve blood
pressure and sustained greater weight loss has the potential to reduce
the need for antihypertensive medications.\
\
Advise that a combination of lifestyle modifications works better than a
single intervention.\
\
Clinical judgement should be used when using BMI for targets in adults
that are highly muscular and in some populations, such as Asian and
older populations, and/or those with additional co-morbidities, and/or
risk factors that may be of concern at different BMIs.\
\
Emphasise that there is no quick solution; lifestyle changes must be
practical and able to be maintained for a lifetime.\
\
targets: Waist circumference \<94 cm males \<90 cm Asian males \<80 cm
females BMI \<25 kg/m2

**\
Diet**\
Diet (fruit and vegetables, fat and salt) Total fat account for 20--35%
of energy intake Salt to ≤6 g/day for primary prevention and 4 g/day for
secondary prevention Five serves of vegetables and two serves of fruit
daily

**\
Smoking Cessation**\
On average, a smoker's life expectancy is up to 10 years less than
non-smokers, and 60% of long-term smokers will die prematurely from a
smoking-related disease. Smoking cessation has been shown to reduce
blood pressure and overall CVD risk.81 In fact, the risk of a coronary
event declines rapidly after quitting and within 2--6 years can be
similar to that of a non-smoker.80 Structured advice from a general
practitioner has been shown to increase cessation rates by two-thirds,
compared with no-advice, and is highly cost effective.82 One such
structured framework is the 5As approach (ask, assesss, advise, assist,
arrange).49, 83 Further information can be found in the Smoking
cessation guidelines for Australian general practice 2014

**\
Alcohol Intake**\
Frequency and volume of alcohol For healthy men and women, drinking no
more than two standard drinks on any day and no more than four on any
one occasion. Consumption of ≤2 standard drinks a day for healthy men
and women can cause an immediate increase in blood pressure, however
this has not been associated with elevated CVD risk.84 In contrast,
consumption of ≥2 standard drinks a day for men and ≥1 standard drinks a
day for women has been found to increase the risk of developing
hypertension
