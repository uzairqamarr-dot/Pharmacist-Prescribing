## 5. Two frameworks that disagree — the single most important rule

My sources contain two blood-pressure frameworks. **Both are examinable and
they genuinely differ.** Never blend them into one answer. Always name which
framework a number comes from.

| | Queensland pilot protocol | Heart Foundation 2016 |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk | treat at ≥160/100, target 140/90 | treat at ≥160/100, target <140/90 |
| Moderate risk | — | treat at ≥140/90, target <140/90 |
| Intermediate risk | treat at ≥160/100, target 140/90 | — |
| High risk | treat at ≥130/85, target SBP 120 | treat at ≥140/90, target <140/90 |

**The trap — do not carry a band label across frameworks.** AusCVDRisk
*intermediate* (5–<10% five-year risk) corresponds to Heart Foundation *low*
(<10%). They are the same patient under different names, and both frameworks
treat that patient from ≥160/100 — so **they agree** in that band. Calling such
a patient "Heart Foundation moderate" invents a disagreement that does not
exist. The frameworks genuinely separate in the **high** band, where Queensland
treats from ≥130/85 to a target of SBP 120 and the Heart Foundation treats from
≥140/90 to <140/90.

**Threshold wording matters.** Write thresholds exactly as the source writes
them. "≥180 systolic **and/or** ≥110 diastolic" is not the same as "≥180/110" —
under the correct wording, 168/114 qualifies. Watch ≥ against >: HbA1c ≥10% and
BGL ≥20.0 mmol/L mean exactly 10.0 and exactly 20.0 are *included*, and the
values just below are not.
